# Finanssi

**Alga's famous Finanssi, the game of the business world, now in a new edition! Join in exciting, fast-paced action – competing for control of business groups through skilful property and share investments. The right decisions require careful planning and sharp reasoning.**

**2–6 players aged over 8.**

## Contents

- Game board
- 2 dice
- 6 playing pieces
- 21 shares
- 20 title deeds
- 12 bonds
- 21 Finance News cards
- 42 Stock Tip cards
- 6 car certificates
- 6 loan certificates
- 8 industrial plants (black) (2 of each kept in reserve)
- 15 buildings (red) (2 of each kept in reserve)
- and a bundle of banknotes of various denominations

## Preparation

a) The cards are carefully detached and sorted into their own piles. The Finance News cards and the Stock Tips, each in their own pile, are shuffled and placed on the board face down.

b) Someone is chosen to be the banker – a player or an outsider. The banker naturally takes care of the bank, sorts the banknotes and places them in the 6 slanted compartments of the box (not the million notes). The banker then gives each player 75,000 in suitable denominations.

c) The players choose their pieces and place them on the bank exit square (no. 1). Everyone rolls both dice. The player with the highest total starts. Play proceeds clockwise.

## Rules

### Movement

Players move clockwise around the board. A player without a car rolls one die; a player with a car rolls two dice. **Exception:** inside the bank, squares 34–46, all players always roll only one die.

Pieces are moved according to the number rolled on the die/dice. **Exception:** a player must always stop on square 34 (bank entrance) and on square 1 (bank exit), even if the roll would allow a longer move.

### Buying a car

On their turn a player may buy a car at any time they choose – the purchase must, however, take place before rolling the dice. The price is 50,000, and the bank issues a car certificate for the purchase. Car owners are entitled to roll two dice (but not inside the bank). A player may own only one car at a time. Trading cars between players is forbidden.

At any time during their turn, either before or after rolling, a player may sell the car back to the bank for 25,000.

### Buying properties and shares

A player may buy properties and shares only while on square 11 (branch office) or on squares 35–46 (head office). Purchases must be made on the player's own turn and **before** rolling the dice. Only one property or share may be bought at a time. If a player has bought a property or share on their turn, they may also buy on their next turn if they are on a square that permits buying and have enough money.

### Building on properties

A player may build on properties they own only while on square 17 or 40 (Rakennusprojekti Oy – "Construction Project Ltd"), or if they have a Stock Tip that allows building at any time. Construction may only be done on the player's own turn and before rolling the dice. The player chooses which of their properties to build on (the properties do not all need to be in the same group). Any number of buildings may be built during the same turn.

The prices of industrial plants and other buildings are shown on the title deeds. Once the player has paid the bank, they receive either an industrial plant (black; on squares 26, 27, 29, 30, 32 and 33) or another building (red; all other squares), which they place on the square to show that it has been built on.

Once a property has been built on, the building cannot later be removed, and the money cannot be recovered from the bank.

### Rent

When a player lands on a property they own, or on a property that has not yet been sold, nothing special happens.

If a player lands on a square (built or unbuilt) owned by another player, they must pay that player rent. The amount of rent is shown on the title deed. **NOTE!** If the property owner forgets to ask for rent and the next player has already rolled the dice, the owner may not claim the rent afterwards and forfeits it.

### Mortgaging properties

If a player has a property that can be mortgaged, they may at any time during their turn (before or after rolling) mortgage one or more of their properties. The bank then pays out the money, and the mortgage is marked by turning the title deed face down.

No rent is collected on a mortgaged property.

A mortgaged property is redeemed during the player's turn, **before** rolling the dice. The redemption price is the mortgage value + 10%.

Players may not grant mortgages to each other.

### Selling properties back

At any time during their turn (before or after rolling) a player may sell one or more properties or shares back to the bank. The buy-back value is shown on the title deed. A mortgaged property cannot be sold on; it must first be redeemed.

### Bank loans

At any time during their turn (before or after rolling, but not on square 43) a player may take one or more loans from the bank. Each loan is 50,000. A player may have at most three loans at the same time. In total, the bank can grant at most six loans.

A player with loans must pay interest on them (5,000 per loan) **every time** they land on square no. 1.

At any time during their turn (before or after rolling) a player may repay their loans. If a player lands on square 43, they must repay one loan (if they have any) and additionally pay the bank 5,000 interest on the repaid loan. A player on square 43 cannot take a new loan.

### Buying bonds

If a player lands on square 45 or 46, they may, if they wish, buy one of the bonds remaining in the bank. If a player draws the Finance News card "Grand Bond Draw", they may buy one bond first, after which all other players, in turn order, may each buy one bond.

Each bond costs 500. A bond that has won a prize must be returned to the bank. Trading bonds between players is not allowed.

### Finance News

A player must draw a new Finance News card (the top card) if they land on either Finance News square or receive a Stock Tip instructing them to do so. The Finance News instruction applies to all players unless stated otherwise. The instruction is read aloud. If the card says "in effect until further notice", it is kept on display until a new Finance News card is drawn. Otherwise the card is placed face down at the bottom of the pile once everyone has first followed its instructions.

### Stock Tip

A player who lands on a Stock Tip square draws the top card from the Stock Tip pile. The card's instructions are followed immediately unless stated otherwise. The card is then placed back at the bottom of the pile.

### Bank entrance

A player must always stop on this square (34), even if the roll would allow a longer move. On landing here, the player immediately rolls both dice (this also applies to players without a car) to determine the size of their reward. The bank pays 5,000 per pip. When the Finance News card "Good Times" is in effect, the reward is doubled. When the Finance News card "Bad Times" is in effect, the reward is paid only according to the die showing the lower number.

### Bank exit

A player must always stop on this square (1), even if the roll would allow a longer move. On landing here, the player immediately draws a Stock Tip and follows its instructions.

### Bank dividend

If a player lands on the dividend square, or if a drawn Stock Tip or Finance News card announces a dividend, the bank pays dividends on shares. The amount is determined case by case.

### Dividends between players

On landing on square 41, or on drawing the corresponding Stock Tip, a player pays their fellow players a 20% dividend on the shares in the groups in which the paying player owns one or more properties.

The board has seven business groups (Käsiteollisuus Oy – "Handicrafts Ltd", Palveluyhtiö – "Service Company", Liikekeskus Oy – "Shopping Centre Ltd", Finanssiyhtymä – "Finance Group", Tekniikka Oy – "Technology Ltd", Kemia Oy – "Chemicals Ltd" and Teollisuuskonserni – "Industrial Conglomerate"). Each group consists of two or three properties of the same colour and a corresponding number of shares.<!-- last line partly illegible in the source image -->

**Example:** Player A owns a property in Käsiteollisuus Oy and in Finanssiyhtymä, as well as two properties in Tekniikka Oy. A lands on square 41 and pays the other players a 20% dividend on the share capital they hold in those companies. B has two 50,000 mk shares in Käsiteollisuus Oy and a 100,000 mk share in Tekniikka Oy. (B also has other shares in groups in which A has no properties, but B receives no dividend on those.) In the business groups where A owns properties, B holds share capital totalling 200,000. A gives B 20% of this amount, i.e. 40,000.

Player C has two 75,000 mk shares in Finanssiyhtymä and one 75,000 mk share in Tekniikka Oy. C therefore receives 20% of 225,000 mk, i.e. 45,000. A pays this amount to C.

Such dividends between players are paid in business groups where the paying player has one or more properties and the recipients have one or more shares.

### Square 46

When a player lands on square 46 while the Finance News card "Good Times" is in effect, the square's instructions apply to all players (in turn order, clockwise, starting with the player on the square).

### Bond draw

The Grand Bond Draw is held when a Finance News card calls for it. The Small Bond Draw takes place every time a player lands on square 45.

The prizes in the Grand Draw are 100,000, 50,000 and 25,000. The prizes in the Small Draw are 50,000, 25,000 and 15,000.

After bonds have been bought according to the rules, the banker rolls both dice. The largest prize goes to the bond whose number equals the total of the dice. The second-largest prize goes to the bond whose number equals the higher die. The smallest prize goes to the bond whose number equals the lower die. If both dice show the same number, the smallest prize goes to the bond whose number is one less than the number on the die.

**Example:** If both dice show 4, the winning bonds are 8, 4 and 3, in that order. If both dice show 1, bond no. 12 wins the smallest prize. A winning bond is returned to the bank.

### Jail

On landing on the jail square (24) a player rolls **one die**. On a 1 or 2, the player must miss one turn. On a 3 or 4, the player must miss two turns. On a 5 or 6, the player must miss three turns.

On landing on square 37, "Go directly to jail", the player rolls one die. If the roll is 3 or higher, the square's instruction need not be followed. On a 1 or 2, the player must immediately move to the jail square (24) and roll one die to find out how many turns they must miss (according to the instructions above). **Exception:** if the player comes directly from jail (without having passed through square 1), the instructions of square 37 do not apply. If the player has left jail and passed through square 1, the instructions of square 37 apply.

Collecting rent, taking part in bond draws and receiving dividends continue as normal even while a player is in jail.

### Bankruptcy

If a player cannot meet their payments using bank loans, mortgages and/or selling back to the bank, they go bankrupt and are out of the game. All their properties, shares, etc. are returned to the bank. Any buildings on their properties are removed.

### Business group

A complete business group consists of all properties of the same colour together with the shares belonging to that group. It makes no difference whether the properties are built on or not.

There are 7 business groups on the board (see "Dividends between players"). Each group has as many shares as it has properties.

**If a player owns a complete business group (all properties and shares of that group), they may charge double rent.**

The simplest way to acquire a complete business group is to buy all its properties and shares from the bank. Usually, however, the properties and shares are held by different players.

To buy an entire business group and thereby gain the right to double the rent, a player must first own at least one property or share in the group, and second, must call a **shareholders' meeting**.

Important: some Stock Tip cards carry the text "does not apply to shares in business groups". This refers to the properties and corresponding shares in a **complete** business group – if a player has such a group, its properties and shares cannot, for example, be forced into a compulsory sale.<!-- last line partly illegible in the source image -->

### Shareholders' meeting

If the player whose turn it is is in the bank's head office (squares 35–46), they may call a shareholders' meeting. The meeting must be called before rolling the dice. The player says, for example, "I call a shareholders' meeting of Tekniikka Oy."

First, the value (purchase price) of the properties and shares owned by the opponents in that company is added up, together with any buildings. The player must now show an equal sum of money as proof of their ability to buy.

Next, the player puts **additional** money on the table – an amount exactly divisible by 10,000 (minimum 20,000, maximum 120,000). This is the **brokerage fee**.

The player then rolls both dice. If the total of the dice is **equal to or less than** the brokerage fee divided by 10,000, the player has won and takes over the shares and properties held by the other players in that group, paying them the purchase prices.

Of the brokerage fee, 30,000 goes to the bank, and the rest is divided equally (rounded to the nearest 500) among the players who sold their properties and shares.

If the total of the dice is greater than the brokerage fee divided by 10,000, the player's takeover attempt fails. The other players keep their properties and shares, and the brokerage fee goes to the bank.

**Example:** A has a 75,000 share as well as a furniture shop and a grocery shop in Liikekeskus Oy. B has a bookshop (a built property) and one 50,000 share. C has the other 50,000 share in Liikekeskus Oy.

A wants the entire business group and calls a shareholders' meeting while in the bank. A adds up B's and C's holdings in Liikekeskus Oy: 20,000 + 20,000 + 50,000 + 50,000 = 140,000. A puts down a brokerage fee of, say, 80,000. Finally, A rolls both dice.

If A rolls 9 or more, the takeover **fails.** B and C keep their properties and shares. A keeps the 140,000, but the entire brokerage fee (80,000 in this case) goes to the bank. If A rolls 8 or less, the takeover **succeeds.** A takes B's property + building + share in Liikekeskus Oy and pays B 90,000. A also takes C's share and pays C 50,000. Of the brokerage fee, 30,000 goes to the bank, and the remaining 50,000 is split equally between B and C. As owner of Liikekeskus Oy, A may now charge double rent.

B and C could equally have made the same takeover, had they had enough money and luck with the dice.

If a player puts down a brokerage fee of 120,000, there is no need to roll the dice – the total can never exceed 12. The takeover then succeeds automatically. Of course, it is not always possible to put down 120,000 as the brokerage fee – but sometimes you have to take a risk to get ahead of the other players! Before calling a shareholders' meeting, a player may, during the same turn, mortgage properties or sell properties or shares back to the bank and/or take out bank loans (one or more). The player does all this to raise funds before the shareholders' meeting.

## Who wins?

The first player to accumulate one million marks (1,000,000) **in cash** while simultaneously owning **at least** two complete business groups wins the game.

It is perfectly allowed to sell or mortgage in order to raise the million marks, but the player must retain ownership of at least two business groups.

If all other players go bankrupt, the last player remaining in the game is the winner.

## Additional tips

In Finanssi, a player plans their strategy right from the start – relying on luck alone will not bring success. Is it wise to invest more in properties or in shares? Is it worth getting involved in business groups owned by others? Will you focus on investing in one business group or in several? How much cash do you need? Is it worth taking out a loan to raise extra capital? How large a brokerage fee are you willing to pay? The more money you use, the better your chances of acquiring business groups – but at the same time, your opponents receive more money to use.

Good luck certainly plays its part in this game too, but in the long run it is skilful, well-planned investments that lead to a surer victory.
